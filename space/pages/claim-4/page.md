# Claim 4 — Proposition 4: explanations are informational garblings

**Verdict: VERIFIED. Confidence: HIGH.**

> Proposition 4 shows that idealized rational agents gain no additional value
> from explanations beyond what is already contained in features and model
> predictions, since explanations are informational garblings of these
> signals (Proposition 4).

Proposition 4 (verbatim): for an explanation Z generated by a function of
(X, Ŷ), "gaining access to the explanation does not improve the expected
performance of the idealized agent, i.e., R_{X∪Ŷ∪Z} = R_{X∪Ŷ}." With
Ŷ = h(X) (the model prediction is itself a function of the features) this
extends to R_{X∪Ŷ∪Z} = R_{X∪Ŷ} = R_X — the form the paper demonstrates
empirically in Appendix B.

This is a universally quantified structural theorem, so we verify its exact
content, not a sampled illustration:

**Independently reconstructed derivation.** R_V = Σ_v max_a Σ_s p(s,v)·u(a,s)
depends on the signal V only through the partition V induces on signal
realizations. The map (x, ŷ) ↦ (x, ŷ, g(x, ŷ)) is injective with the
projection as its inverse — a garbling in both directions, exactly the
paper's two-sided Blackwell argument (Theorem 5) — so (X,Ŷ,Z) and (X,Ŷ)
induce the *same* partition, hence the same posteriors, hence equal R. When
Ŷ = h(X), the same argument collapses both to the partition induced by X
alone.

**Part A — exhaustive decision procedure.** For *every* garbling g on
|X|=3, |Ŷ|=2, |Z|=2 (all 64), the partition identity is checked exactly; and
with fully symbolic p(s,x,ŷ) and u(a,s) (sympy), the grouping procedure
derives R_XYZ ≡ R_XY for every g — i.e. **for all priors and all utilities**
on those alphabets, not a numeric sample.

**Part B — exact rational arithmetic.** 500 random decision problems
(Fraction-valued p, u; random h, g; |S|,|A|,|X|,|Ŷ|,|Z| ≤ 4):
R_XYZ == R_XY == R_X holds as exact rational equalities, zero tolerance,
500/500.

**Part C — negative control.** A state-peeking pseudo-explanation (not a
function of (x,ŷ)) strictly increases R (1/2 → 1): the check fails exactly
when the garbling assumption fails, as the paper's Section 3.2 predicts for
non-idealized settings.

**Full-scale empirical arm (the paper's own Appendix-B demonstration).** On
the Ames case study, the gain of Z over X∪Ŷ is
-1.21% (CI
[-1.52, -0.91]), of Ŷ over X
-3.12% (CI
[-3.58, -2.67]) — never significantly positive:
empirically, explanations and model predictions offer **no additional value
over the features**, matching the paper's Figure-1 finding. (The small
negative means are the finite-sample cost of redundant inputs to the
empirical agent; the exact rational agent of Parts A–B shows strict
equality.)
